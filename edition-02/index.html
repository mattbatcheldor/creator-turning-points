Why this edition exists

Edition 01 showed that the most famous creator moments mostly prove attention. Edition 02 goes looking for proof: sales data, retailer figures, company results and published research. Most cases here have numbers beyond views. A survey and a livestream record also test the limits of that evidence. The question is how much of the change belongs to the creator.

01 / The finding

The numbers are clearer than the attribution.

Thirteen of these fifteen cases carry a Sales trend rating. That group includes Pinch of Nom’s 210,506 launch copies, Little Moons’ 700% Tesco increase and Colleen Hoover’s 14.3 million print books. It also includes company-wide revenue, shipments and a share-price fall, which are different kinds of commercial record.

One case rests on a survey of 1,500 teenagers. One rests on a livestream record of 955,185 concurrent viewers. None has a modelled effect. The cases record a change alongside creator work, but do not isolate the creator’s contribution from distribution, other creators or wider events.

The most useful evidence often comes from the partner who fulfils the demand: booksellers, grocery platforms, retailers and company finance teams. The Conspiracy palette figure comes from the creators themselves and is labelled as a claim. For a new programme, I’d agree the outcome, baseline and reporting window with the creator and delivery partner before the work starts.

01 Marques Brownlee / 2024 / TrustThe Worst Product I’ve Ever ReviewedA company raised more than $200 million for an AI assistant. Brownlee tested the $699 device and told his audience where it failed.YouTube · Product failed About 10,000Ai Pins and accessories shipped by August 2024, as reportedSales trend

Kearny, United States / Technology reviews

Marques Brownlee, 2024

The situation

Humane had spent years and more than $200 million building the Ai Pin, a wearable AI assistant meant to replace the phone. Its launch depended on early reviews persuading people to pay $699 plus a monthly subscription.

The move

Marques Brownlee, known as MKBHD, tested the device for his channel and titled his review The Worst Product I’ve Ever Reviewed... For Now. He listed what failed, from slow answers to a projector that was hard to see outdoors.

  • Marques Brownlee Fifteen years of reviews and an audience that trusts his verdict on hardware.
  • Humane An ambitious product that wasn’t ready for the scrutiny.

The turning point

The review in April 2024 set off a public debate about whether one creator should hold that much power. By August, reporting showed returns outpacing sales. In February 2025 HP bought Humane’s technology for $116 million and the pins stopped working.

My take

This is the clearest modern example of a creator acting like a critic with the reach of a broadcaster. The debate about fairness misses the point: his audience follows him because he says what he thinks.

Reporting records the shipment shortfall and returns. The count includes accessories. Other reviewers also criticised the product, and the review’s separate contribution to demand was not measured.

What to take into a creator programme

A trusted reviewer is not a channel you can buy. If the product isn’t ready, the most credible creators will say so, and their audience will listen.

How I’d measure it now

For review-led launches, compare pre-orders and returns in markets and segments where a review was widely watched with those where it wasn’t, and track search sentiment for the product in the week after.

02 Smosh and the YouTubers / 2014 / TrustThe Survey That Ranked YouTubers Above Film StarsVariety asked 1,500 American teenagers who they admired. The top five were all YouTubers.YouTube · Survey published Top 5All five most influential figures for US teens were YouTubersSurvey

Sacramento, United States / Online video and celebrity

Variety, 2014

The situation

In 2014 brands and studios still treated YouTube stars as niche. Advertising budgets followed television ratings and film stars, and most marketers had not heard of the creators teenagers watched every day.

The move

Variety commissioned a survey of 1,500 Americans aged 13 to 18, scoring ten YouTube creators and ten traditional stars on qualities such as approachability and authenticity.

  • The creators Years of frequent, personal uploads that made teenagers feel they knew them.
  • Variety A trade title that put the finding in front of the people who set budgets.

The turning point

Smosh came first, followed by The Fine Bros, PewDiePie, KSI and Ryan Higa. The highest traditional star was Paul Walker, in sixth. The ranking became the most quoted statistic in early creator marketing pitches.

My take

This survey did more to unlock brand budgets than any single video. It gave marketers a number to put on a slide, and the number said the old celebrity model was losing teenagers.

What the evidence shows

The survey design, sample and ranking are reported by Tubefilter and others. It shows how teenagers felt about creators, but it cannot show that the feeling turned into sales, and the list of stars tested was chosen by the publisher.

What to take into a creator programme

Familiarity builds trust. Creators who show up often and talk directly to their audience earn a kind of credibility that a famous face in an advert doesn’t.

How I’d measure it now

Pair a trust survey with behaviour: ask the same audience which products they bought on a creator’s recommendation, and check a sample against sales data from tracked links.

03 Mrs Hinch / 2018 / TrustThe Hinch EffectA hairdresser filmed herself cleaning her house. Shops ran out of disinfectant.Instagram · Shelves cleared +28%Zoflora sales growth, 2018 year to dateSales trend

Maldon, United Kingdom / Household cleaning

Sophie Hinchliffe, 2018

The situation

Cleaning products were sold through supermarket promotions and television advertising, and nobody thought of cleaning as content people would choose to watch.

The move

Sophie Hinchliffe posted Instagram stories of her cleaning routines, naming the cheap products she used and giving them nicknames. Her followers, who called themselves Hinchers, went out and bought them.

  • Sophie Hinchliffe Daily routines, warmth and product names her followers could copy.
  • The Hinchers A community that shared hauls and cleared shelves of the products she used.

The turning point

In 2018 retailers reported shortages of products she used. Zoflora, a disinfectant she favoured, doubled production to almost a million bottles a week and reported sales up 28% for the year. Her first book followed in 2019 and became a bestseller.

My take

Mrs Hinch made cleaning a spectator sport and turned cheap products into collectables. It is also a reminder that the brands that benefit most from a creator sometimes never say her name.

04 Kylie Jenner / 2018 / TrustSooo Does Anyone Else Not Open Snapchat Anymore?One of Snapchat’s biggest users said she had stopped opening it. The company lost $1.3 billion in a day.Snapchat · Shares fell $1.3 billionEstimated market value lost in one daySales trend

Los Angeles, United States / Social media

Kylie Jenner, 2018

The situation

Snapchat had redesigned its app in late 2017, and many users disliked it. A petition to reverse the change had around a million signatures. Investors were already nervous.

The move

On 21 February 2018 Kylie Jenner, then one of the platform’s most followed users, posted asking whether anyone else had stopped opening Snapchat. She followed up minutes later to say she still loved it.

  • Kylie Jenner An audience in the tens of millions and a personal verdict on the app.
  • Snap’s investors A share price already under pressure from an analyst downgrade earlier that week.

The turning point

Snap’s shares fell about 6% the next day, an estimated $1.3 billion of market value, and the story went round the world as proof of influencer power.

My take

The number is probably overstated, but the lesson isn’t. When the people who make a platform interesting say they are bored, investors listen.

05 Kate Allinson and Kay Featherstone / 2019 / CrossoverThe Fastest-Selling Non-Fiction BookTwo food bloggers built a community around everyday recipes. Their first cookbook set a Nielsen BookScan launch record.Blog · Book record 210,506Copies sold at launch, a Nielsen BookScan record for non-fictionSales trend

Wirral, United Kingdom / Food and publishing

Kate Allinson and Kay Featherstone, 2019

The situation

Kate Allinson and Kay Featherstone had built an audience around recipes people could use at home. A cookbook had to give those readers enough new, tested material to justify paying for it.

The move

Kate Allinson and Kay Featherstone built Pinch of Nom, a free website and social community of lower-calorie recipes, then published a cookbook with Bluebird that their audience had been asking for.

  • Kate Allinson and Kay Featherstone Tested recipes and a large, loyal community that already cooked them every week.
  • Bluebird Print, distribution and a launch pushed through supermarkets and bookshops.

The turning point

The book sold 210,506 copies in its first days on sale in March 2019, the fastest-selling non-fiction title since Nielsen BookScan records began. It later passed a million copies.

My take

I see the book as an extension of a useful relationship. Readers already cooked the recipes and helped test them. Bluebird gave that practice a paid format and wider distribution.

City not disclosed, Australia / Mobile gaming

Plarium and creators, 2019 to 2022

The situation

Mobile games competed for players through app store adverts and performance marketing. Creator sponsorships were used, but rarely as the main engine of growth.

The move

After its worldwide launch in February 2019, Plarium paid thousands of YouTube creators, from the biggest to the mid-sized, to read sponsored segments for Raid: Shadow Legends, many with a tracked link and a signing bonus. Internet Historian made the sponsor read part of the entertainment.

  • Internet Historian and other creators Audience knowledge and a way to make a familiar sponsor message part of their programmes.
  • Plarium Acquisition budget, tracked offers and repeat sponsorships.

The turning point

The sponsorships became a meme that creators themselves joked about. By July 2022 the game had passed $1 billion in player spending on mobile, with the United States its biggest market.

My take

I’d ask which creator audiences bring players who stay. Internet Historian’s integrated sponsorship shows that delivery can be a creative choice. The billion-dollar total still cannot tell me what that choice earned.

07 Shane Dawson and Jeffree Star / 2019 / FormatThe Conspiracy CollectionA YouTube documentary series showed a palette being made. It sold out the moment it launched.YouTube · Sold out 1 millionPalettes sold in about 30 minutes, as claimed by the creatorsSales trend

Los Angeles, United States / Beauty and online video

Shane Dawson and Jeffree Star, 2019

The situation

Make-up launches relied on teaser posts and influencer seeding. Few brands showed customers how a product was developed, priced and argued over.

The move

Shane Dawson made The Beautiful World of Jeffree Star, a multi-part series following the development of a palette with the make-up entrepreneur Jeffree Star, including disagreements about cost and design.

  • Shane Dawson A documentary format, a huge audience and years of long-form storytelling.
  • Jeffree Star A beauty company able to make and ship the product at scale.

The turning point

The Conspiracy collection launched on 1 November 2019 and the site struggled under the demand. The pair said a million palettes sold within about 30 minutes, and a restock followed.

My take

The series turned the launch into television, and the audience paid to see the ending. It is a strong format and a fragile one, because it ties a product to two personalities.

Honolulu, United States / Skincare

Hyram Yarbro, 2020

The situation

CeraVe was a dermatologist-developed brand sold in pharmacies, trusted but unglamorous. Younger shoppers bought skincare from beauty brands with big marketing budgets.

The move

Hyram Yarbro, known as Skincare by Hyram, reviewed products and routines on YouTube and then TikTok, praising affordable, simple products and criticising expensive ones. CeraVe was among his favourites.

The turning point

During 2020 his following grew into the millions and CeraVe became a TikTok fixture. L’Oréal reported that CeraVe had almost doubled in size that year, and its general manager credited Yarbro’s support with bringing the brand major attention.

My take

This is the case for making products that hold up to a sceptic. CeraVe didn’t buy Hyram’s praise. It earned it, then later worked with him openly.

09 Joe Wicks / 2020 / LivePE With JoeSchools closed overnight. A fitness coach became the nation’s PE teacher at 9am every weekday.YouTube · Record 955,185Concurrent viewers of the 24 March 2020 lesson, a Guinness World RecordReach only

London, United Kingdom / Fitness and education

Joe Wicks, 2020

The situation

When the UK went into lockdown in March 2020, millions of children were suddenly at home with no school day and no exercise routine. Public broadcasters were still planning their response.

The move

Joe Wicks, The Body Coach, streamed a free 30-minute workout for children live on YouTube every weekday morning, starting on 23 March 2020, and said he would give the advertising income to NHS charities.

  • Joe Wicks A daily routine, energy and a channel already built for workouts.
  • Families A captive audience looking for structure, sharing the stream with schools and friends.

The turning point

The first lesson drew 2.3 million views in its first nine hours. The second, on 24 March, set a Guinness World Record for the most viewers of a fitness live stream on YouTube.

My take

PE With Joe is the public service moment of the creator economy. It did what a broadcaster would have done, faster, and earned Wicks a national standing no advertising campaign could buy.

London, United Kingdom / Frozen desserts

TikTok users and Little Moons, 2021

The situation

Little Moons, a London mochi brand founded by siblings Howard and Vivien Wong, was a small supermarket line with modest awareness and no big advertising budget.

The move

In January 2021 TikTok users began posting videos of themselves searching supermarkets for Little Moons, tasting them and rating the flavours. The brand had not paid for the trend and moved quickly to support it.

  • TikTok users Thousands of videos that turned a frozen dessert into a lockdown treasure hunt.
  • Little Moons A product that looked good on camera, and a team that leaned into the moment.

The turning point

Tesco reported sales up more than 700% in a week against an average December week, Waitrose 350% over two days, and online grocers sold out. Videos tagged with the brand passed 19.5 million views.

My take

Little Moons shows a creator moment with no creator in charge: thousands of ordinary users made the ad. The brand’s best decision was to keep the shelves full and say thank you.

The situation

Recipes spread through food magazines, cookery programmes and big recipe sites. A home cook in Finland had no obvious route to an American audience.

The move

Jenni Häyrinen published a recipe for pasta tossed with feta baked alongside cherry tomatoes, adapted from another Finnish cook. It became so popular in Finland that shops sold out of feta. In early 2021 American food creators made it on TikTok.

In February 2021 American grocers reported feta selling out. The Fresh Market chain said its feta sales were up 45%.

I like that the original creator is a Finnish blogger, not an American TikTok star. The internet often forgets where a trend began. This archive shouldn’t.

05 / The evidence

What creator proof can and can’t show

Moment, milestone, result

A first upload or a record is a moment. A milestone, like a subscriber count, shows attention. Only a result shows what changed for the creator, the platform or a brand. Each case says which of the three the record supports.

Whose number it is

Many figures come from the platforms, the creators themselves or the press. The source for every outcome is named, so you can judge it. Estimates of creator income are marked as estimates.

Attention is not income

Views, subscribers and concurrent viewers count attention. Paid subscriptions, sales and chart positions get closer to money. The rating says which kind of number a case rests on.

The evidence rating

Every case gets one of five ratings, strongest first: modelled effect, sales trend, survey, reach only and archival. A date or a deal term is marked as a legacy fact so it isn’t read as a result.

The creator comes first

Each case puts the creator at the centre: what they made, what changed because of it, and who else was involved. Brand partnerships appear when the creator drove them.

How cases earn a place

Each case changed what was possible for creators, platforms or brands, and left a public record. Creator stories already told in Brand Turning Points or Partnership Turning Points are not repeated here; The completed Index will link to them.
