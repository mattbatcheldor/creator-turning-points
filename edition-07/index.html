Why this edition exists

Ninety cases in, most of this archive happened in Britain and the United States. Edition 07 closes the series with ten that didn’t. They come from nine countries on four continents, and several were made with a phone and no budget at all. Many of the formats the West later adopted, live tipping, virtual creators and shopping inside a stream, were working at scale here first.

01 / The finding

The edge invented formats the centre later copied.

Six of these ten cases rest on reach: 14.1 million subscribers, Africa’s first comedy channel to a million, Southeast Asia’s first ten million and TikTok’s most followed person. Two get close to money: $9,000 a month from viewers’ gifts in 2014, and $1.7 billion sold in twelve hours in 2021. Two are archival.

Three of the formats here travelled to the West: live gifts on a Korean eating stream, virtual creators from Japan and live shopping from China. In each case the idea was working at scale years before Western platforms built the features to support it.

Five of the ten were made with almost no budget: a phone in Nairobi, a webcam in Seoul, a cousin and a camera in Port Harcourt, a factory worker’s shrug in Turin and a bedroom in Piauí. What they had was a format that needed no translation.

01 Li Ziqi / 2021 / FormatThe Village KitchenA young woman filmed herself growing, cooking and making things by hand in rural Sichuan. Without saying a word in English, she reached more subscribers than any Chinese-language channel.YouTube · Record 14.1 millionYouTube subscribers, a Guinness World Record for a Chinese-language channelReach only

Sichuan, China / Food and rural life

Li Ziqi, 2016 to 2021

The situation

Chinese creators were rarely seen outside China, because the biggest Chinese platforms are separate from the West’s. Rural life was not seen as glamorous content.

The move

Li Ziqi began filming in 2016, at first on her own, making slow, beautiful films about traditional food and crafts in her village with her grandmother. They had almost no dialogue, so they needed no translation.

  • Li Ziqi Skill, patience and films that felt like paintings, made with very little at the start.
  • Her audience Viewers across the world who found calm in her work, and Chinese platforms where she was even bigger.

The turning point

In January 2021 Guinness World Records recognised her channel as having the most subscribers for a Chinese-language YouTube channel, at 14.1 million.

My take

Li Ziqi is the case I’d show anyone who thinks the creator economy is American. A woman in a Sichuan village made some of the most beautiful content on the platform, and the world found her.

02 The Diva / 2014 / MonetisationMukbangA woman in Seoul ate dinner on a live stream every night. Viewers sent virtual gifts worth thousands of dollars a month.AfreecaTV · Reported earnings $9,000Reported monthly earnings from viewers’ gifts, 2014Sales trend

Seoul, South Korea / Food and live streaming

Park Seo-yeon, 2014

The situation

In South Korea many people lived and ate alone. Live streaming platforms like AfreecaTV let viewers send hosts virtual items that converted to cash.

The move

Park Seo-yeon, broadcasting as The Diva, ate large meals live while chatting to viewers, who kept her company and she kept them company. The format was called mukbang, from the Korean for eating broadcast.

  • The Diva Hours of live company over a shared meal.
  • AfreecaTV Virtual gifts, called star balloons, that let viewers pay the host directly.

The turning point

In 2014 international media reported she earned about $9,000 a month from viewers’ gifts. Mukbang spread worldwide onto YouTube and became one of the platform’s best-known formats.

My take

Mukbang shows that live tipping, now common everywhere, was a mature business in Korea first. The West copied the format and the payment model.

03 Kizuna AI / 2016 / FormatThe First Virtual YouTuberAn animated girl introduced herself as a virtual YouTuber. She started a genre with millions of fans worldwide.YouTube · Genre created December 2016When Kizuna AI launched · legacy factArchival

Tokyo, Japan / Animation and online video

Kizuna AI, 2016

The situation

YouTube stars were people on camera. Japanese animation and motion capture were sophisticated, but no one had combined them into a creator persona.

The move

In December 2016 a Japanese company launched Kizuna AI, an animated character voiced and performed live by a real person, who called herself a virtual YouTuber and made videos like any other creator.

  • Kizuna AI’s team A character, a voice and motion capture that let the avatar move naturally.
  • Her fans An audience that embraced a creator who was a character.

The turning point

The term VTuber spread, and agencies such as Hololive built rosters of virtual creators whose live streams and fan gifts became one of YouTube’s biggest categories.

My take

VTubers are the creator economy’s most original invention outside the West. They also raise the ownership question in its sharpest form: the performer or the company?

04 Whindersson Nunes / 2016 / DiscoveryBrazil’s Biggest YouTuberA young man from one of Brazil’s poorest states made comedy videos in his room. He became the most subscribed YouTuber in the country.YouTube · Most subscribed in Brazil No. 1Most subscribed YouTube channel in Brazil, 2016Reach only

Piauí, Brazil / Comedy

Whindersson Nunes, 2013 to 2016

The situation

Brazilian entertainment was dominated by television networks in Rio and São Paulo. A comedian from the north-east had little route onto national television.

The move

Whindersson Nunes began posting comedy and music parodies about everyday life in his home region, in his own accent and humour.

  • Whindersson Nunes Relatable comedy and a voice from a region television ignored.
  • Brazilian viewers One of YouTube’s largest national audiences, eager for creators who sounded like them.

The turning point

In 2016 his channel became the most subscribed in Brazil and one of the most subscribed in the world, leading to sold-out live tours and film roles.

My take

Whindersson is what happens when the gatekeepers are bypassed. Brazil’s biggest comedian came from the place its television never looked.

05 Mark Angel Comedy / 2017 / DiscoveryAfrica’s First MillionA Nigerian filmmaker shot short sketches on a small budget with his family. His was the first African comedy channel to a million subscribers.YouTube · Milestone 1 millionSubscribers, the first African comedy channel to get there, 2017Reach only

Port Harcourt, Nigeria / Comedy

Mark Angel, 2017

The situation

Nigeria had a huge film industry, Nollywood, but African creators were barely visible on YouTube’s global charts, and advertising rates in Africa were low.

The move

Mark Angel wrote and filmed short comedy sketches in Port Harcourt, often starring his young cousin Emmanuella, whose comic timing made the videos spread across Africa and beyond.

  • Mark Angel Writing, direction and a habit of short, punchy sketches.
  • His cast and audience Performers audiences loved, and viewers across Africa and its diaspora.

The turning point

In 2017 Mark Angel Comedy became the first African comedy channel to reach a million subscribers, and later one of the most subscribed channels on the continent.

My take

Mark Angel proved that an African creator could build a global audience from a phone camera and a good script. Many African creators followed.

06 Atta Halilintar / 2019 / CommunitySoutheast Asia’s First Ten MillionAn Indonesian vlogger from a family of eleven siblings became the first person in Southeast Asia with ten million YouTube subscribers.YouTube · Milestone 10 millionSubscribers, the first in Southeast Asia, February 2019Reach only

Jakarta, Indonesia / Entertainment

Atta Halilintar, 2019

The situation

Indonesia has one of the world’s largest online populations, but its creators were rarely counted among YouTube’s leaders.

The move

Atta Halilintar posted vlogs, pranks and family content, including his large family known as Gen Halilintar, building a fanbase he called ASHIAP Nation.

  • Atta Halilintar Constant uploads, a named fan community and family members who were creators too.
  • Indonesian viewers A vast, young and fast-growing audience on mobile.

The turning point

In February 2019 he became the first person in Southeast Asia to reach ten million subscribers, and passed twenty million within a year.

My take

Atta is a reminder that the biggest creator in a market of 270 million people may be someone most Western marketers have never heard of.

07 CarryMinati / 2020 / CommunityYouTube vs TikTokAn Indian YouTuber roasted a TikTok star. YouTube removed the video, and his fans took it out on TikTok’s app rating.YouTube · Removed DaysHow quickly the video became one of India’s most viewed, and was removedReach only

Faridabad, India / Comedy

CarryMinati, 2020

The situation

In 2020 India’s YouTubers and TikTokers were in a public rivalry over which platform’s creators were more talented.

The move

Ajey Nagar, known as CarryMinati, posted a roast of a TikTok creator in May 2020 that became one of the most viewed and liked videos in India within days.

  • CarryMinati Fast, loud roasts in Hindi, and one of India’s largest YouTube audiences.
  • His fans A community that acted in his defence when the video came down.

The turning point

YouTube removed the video for breaching its harassment rules. His fans responded with a campaign of one-star reviews that pushed TikTok’s Google Play rating down sharply until Google removed millions of reviews.

My take

This case shows the power and the danger of a mobilised fanbase, a month before India banned TikTok altogether, in Edition 04.

Nairobi, Kenya / Comedy

Elsa Majimbo, 2020

The situation

Global fashion brands rarely found talent in Nairobi, and comedy stardom usually needed a studio, an agent and a television slot.

The move

Elsa Majimbo posted short, deadpan monologues filmed on her phone during lockdown in 2020, often wearing sunglasses and eating crisps, mocking everyday life and herself.

  • Elsa Majimbo A distinct voice and videos that cost almost nothing to make.
  • Global brands Fenty, then others, who wanted her humour and her audience.

In September 2020 she signed a deal with Fenty, followed by work with luxury brands and an E! People’s Choice Awards nomination.

My take

Elsa Majimbo is the smallest budget in the archive and one of the fastest routes to global brands. She is what the creator economy promised everyone.

Shanghai, China / Livestream shopping

Austin Li, 2021

The situation

In the West, influencer marketing meant posts and links. In China, live shopping streams on Taobao Live had become a main way to buy, with hosts selling to millions in real time.

The move

Li Jiaqi, known as Austin Li and the Lipstick King after testing hundreds of lipsticks on his own lips, hosted marathon live streams presenting products and negotiating prices for his viewers.

  • Austin Li Product knowledge, energy and a reputation for getting viewers the best price.
  • Taobao Live and brands Checkout inside the stream and discounts brands offered for his audience.

The turning point

During the presale for Singles Day in October 2021 he sold about 10.6 billion yuan, around $1.7 billion, in a single twelve-hour stream, as reported.

My take

Austin Li is the most commercially powerful single creator in this archive. He shows where creator commerce goes when the checkout sits inside the stream.

Khaby Lame, 2020 to 2022

The situation

TikTok’s biggest stars were American teenagers dancing. Khaby Lame, born in Senegal and raised in Italy, had been laid off from a factory job in 2020.

04 / The evidence

What a turning point at the edge can and can’t prove

Moment, milestone, result

A first upload or a record is a moment. A milestone, like a subscriber count, shows attention. Only a result shows what changed for the creator, the platform or a brand. Each case says which of the three the record supports.

Whose number it is

Many figures come from the platforms, the creators themselves or the press. The source for every outcome is named, so you can judge it. Estimates of creator income are marked as estimates.

Attention is not income

Views, subscribers and concurrent viewers count attention. Paid subscriptions, sales and chart positions get closer to money. The rating says which kind of number a case rests on.

The evidence rating

Every case gets one of five ratings, strongest first: modelled effect, sales trend, survey, reach only and archival. A date or a deal term is marked as a legacy fact so it isn’t read as a result.

The creator comes first

Each case puts the creator at the centre: what they made, what changed because of it, and who else was involved. Brand partnerships appear when the creator drove them.

How cases earn a place

Each case changed what was possible for creators, platforms or brands, and left a public record. Creator stories already told in Brand Turning Points or Partnership Turning Points are not repeated here; The Index links to them.

05 / What lasts

A hundred turning points, three lessons.

Across seven editions, the same three things recur. First, creators find the use for a format before the platform does, from Twitch Plays Pokémon to Khaby Lame. Second, attention is easy to count and hard to turn into proof: only a handful of the hundred cases show a result anyone measured properly. Third, the creators who last own something, an audience they can reach directly, a product or a company, so a platform’s decision can’t switch them off.
