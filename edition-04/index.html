Why this edition exists

Editions 01 to 03 followed fame, proof and money. Edition 04 follows the formats themselves: blogs, podcasts, six-second loops, stories, live rooms, always-on streams and worlds built inside games. In almost every case a creator, not the platform, showed what the new format was for.

01 / The finding

Creators show platforms what their formats are for.

Eight of these fifteen cases rest on attention: 1.16 million players, 136 million views, five million downloads, 100,000 people at a virtual club. Seven are archival: a date, a launch, a merger or a ban.

In most of them the platform built the feature and a creator found the use. Facebook paid publishers for live video and a mother in a Chewbacca mask beat them. Snapchat built stories for friends and DJ Khaled made them a show. Twitch built chat and a programmer turned it into a controller.

The other lesson is risk. Vine closed, Clubhouse emptied, a ban cut off India’s TikTok creators overnight and a false claim took down Lofi Girl. Formats move; creators who own their relationship with the audience move with them.

A note on scope

What Edition 04 leans on

All 15 cases run from 2002 to 2021. Ten began in the United States, and one each in Australia, France, China, India and inside Roblox, which is global. The edition covers twelve platforms, because the point is the change of format, not any single app. Edition 07 goes further beyond the usual places.

01 Twitch Plays Pokémon / 2014 / CommunityA Million People, One Game BoyAn anonymous programmer let the chat control the game. More than a million people played the same Pokémon at once.Twitch · Game completed 1.16 millionPeople who sent a command, a Guinness World RecordReach only

Australia / Gaming and live streaming

Anonymous creator, 2014

The situation

Live streams were something you watched. The chat beside them was a place to talk, not to take part in what happened on screen.

The move

In February 2014 an anonymous Australian programmer streamed Pokémon Red on Twitch with a script that turned every chat message such as up or A into a button press, so the whole audience played together.

  • The programmer A simple, open idea and a stream that ran around the clock.
  • The audience Over a million players, plus the jokes, art and legends that grew around their chaos.

The turning point

After 16 days, on 1 March 2014, the crowd finished the game. About 1.16 million people had entered a command and the stream drew around 36 million views, a Guinness World Record for the most people playing a single-player game.

My take

Twitch Plays Pokémon is the purest community case in the archive. Nobody was the star, and that was the point. Every interactive stream and game show on Twitch since owes something to it.

The audience will play if you give them controls. Interactive formats turn viewers into a community faster than anything else.

How I’d measure it now

For an interactive format, measure the share of viewers who take an action, and how long they stay compared with ordinary streams.

02 Heather Armstrong / 2002 / FormatDoocedShe was sacked for writing about work on her blog. Her name became the word for it, and the blog became her job.Blog · Fired, then famous 2002The year she was fired for her blog · legacy factArchival

Los Angeles, United States / Blogging

Heather Armstrong, 2002

The situation

In 2002 blogs were personal sites read by friends and a few strangers. Writing honestly about your life online had no rules, and no one had made it a career.

The move

Heather Armstrong wrote dooce.com, a funny, candid blog that included stories about her workplace. When her employer found it, she was fired.

  • Heather Armstrong Sharp, honest writing about work, depression and later motherhood.
  • Her readers An audience that grew into millions and made the blog a business.

The turning point

To be dooced came to mean being fired for something you wrote online. Armstrong turned the blog into one of the first personal sites to support a family through advertising, and became a founding figure of parenting blogs.

My take

Dooce is where personal life became publishing. Every vlogger, every family channel and every workplace social media policy descends from this moment.

03 Gentle Whispering / 2011 / FormatThe Whisper GenreShe whispered and folded towels on camera. Millions of people used her videos to fall asleep.YouTube · A genre formed 2011When Gentle Whispering began uploading · legacy factArchival

Florida, United States / Online video and wellbeing

Maria Viktorovna, 2011

The situation

Some people felt a calming tingle from soft sounds and close attention, but had no name for it and no content made for it. Online video was built on loud, fast entertainment.

The move

Maria Viktorovna began uploading videos as Gentle Whispering in 2011, whispering, tapping and role-playing quiet, caring scenes designed to trigger what people now call ASMR.

  • Maria Viktorovna Patient, carefully recorded videos and a gentle on-screen manner.
  • The ASMR community Viewers who shared the name, the triggers and their own videos.

The turning point

Her channel became one of the first ASMR channels with a mass audience, and the genre spread into thousands of channels, sleep apps and advertising that borrowed its sounds.

My take

ASMR shows the creator economy finding audiences no broadcaster would have commissioned for. A quiet woman with a good microphone built a genre.

For utility formats, measure repeat viewing per person and time of day. A video watched every night is a different asset from one watched once.

04 King Bach / 2015 / FormatSix SecondsA comedian wrote sketches that lasted six seconds. He became the most followed person on Vine.Vine · Most followed No. 1Most followed account on Vine, March 2015Reach only

Los Angeles, United States / Comedy and short video

King Bach, 2013 to 2016

The situation

Vine, launched in 2013, limited every video to six looping seconds. Most users posted clips of their day. Few thought you could tell a joke with a set-up and a punchline in that time.

The move

Andrew Bachelor, known as King Bach, wrote and acted tightly edited sketches with friends, often featuring other Vine stars, landing a twist in the final second.

  • King Bach Comic timing built for the loop and a stream of collaborations.
  • Vine A format with one rule, and a feed that rewarded whatever made people watch twice.

The turning point

In March 2015 he passed Nash Grier to become the most followed account on Vine. Short-form comedy became a training ground for a generation of creators who moved to TikTok, YouTube and television.

My take

King Bach proved that six seconds was enough for a joke. TikTok inherited Vine’s best creators and its best idea: short, loopable, rewatchable.

05 Serial / 2014 / FormatThe Podcast Everyone Talked AboutA radio journalist re-examined one murder case over twelve episodes. Podcasting suddenly had a hit.Podcast · Record 5 millionDownloads and streams, the fastest to get there on iTunesReach only

Chicago, United States / Podcasting

Sarah Koenig and Julie Snyder, 2014

The situation

Podcasts had existed for a decade but were a niche, mostly radio shows repackaged for download. Few had mass audiences, and advertisers barely noticed them.

The move

Sarah Koenig and the team behind This American Life released Serial, a weekly investigation into a 1999 murder in Baltimore, told one episode at a time with the reporting still in progress.

  • Sarah Koenig and Julie Snyder Investigative reporting, a cliffhanger structure and a voice listeners trusted.
  • Listeners Weekly downloads, theories and conversations that spread the show by word of mouth.

The turning point

In November 2014 Serial became the fastest podcast to reach five million downloads and streams on iTunes. Podcasts became a mainstream format and a market advertisers chased.

My take

Serial is the moment audio became a creator medium worth taking seriously. Every podcast deal in Edition 03 rests on the audience habit it created.

06 DJ Khaled / 2015 / FormatLost at SeaA producer got lost on a jet ski at night and filmed it. Snapchat stories became a stage.Snapchat · Phenomenon December 2015When the jet ski story went viral · legacy factArchival

Miami, United States / Music and social media

DJ Khaled, 2015

The situation

Snapchat’s disappearing stories were used for private moments between friends. Celebrities used polished posts on other platforms and barely touched it.

The move

DJ Khaled posted daily stories full of motivational slogans, which he called keys, and narrated his life as it happened. In December 2015 he filmed himself lost on a jet ski off Miami at night.

  • DJ Khaled Unfiltered energy, catchphrases and a willingness to post everything.
  • Snapchat A format that rewarded the raw, the immediate and the slightly absurd.

The turning point

The jet ski story spread far beyond Snapchat and made Khaled one of the most watched people on the app. His slogans became a book and his profile rose with it, showing celebrities what stories could do.

My take

Khaled turned Snapchat stories into a show before Instagram copied the format. The jet ski is silly, and it taught a generation of celebrities to behave like creators.

07 Candace Payne / 2016 / LiveChewbacca MomA mother laughed in a Chewbacca mask in a car park. It became the most watched Facebook Live video ever.Facebook · Record 136 millionViews within days, a Facebook Live recordReach only

Texas, United States / Social video

Candace Payne, 2016

The situation

Facebook launched Live in 2016 and paid publishers and celebrities to use it. The company wanted a breakthrough moment for the format.

The move

Candace Payne went live from her car to show her children a Chewbacca mask she had bought, and laughed so hard at herself that she could barely speak.

  • Candace Payne Unscripted joy that anyone could share.
  • Facebook A new live feature and a feed that pushed it to friends and their friends.

The turning point

The video passed 136 million views within days, the most for a Facebook Live, and Payne was invited to Facebook’s headquarters and onto television. Facebook named it the top live video of 2016.

My take

Facebook paid media companies millions for live video, and a mother in a car park beat them all for free. It is the clearest example of a platform being taught its own product by a user.

08 Lofi Girl / 2017 / FormatBeats to Relax and Study ToAn animated girl studies by a window while music plays, forever. It was a live stream that never stopped, until a false claim took it down.YouTube · Taken down, then restored About 2 yearsContinuous run of the stream before the false takedown, 2022 · legacy factArchival

France / Music and live streaming

Lofi Girl, 2017 to 2022

The situation

Live streams were events with a start and an end. Music on YouTube meant individual videos and playlists.

The move

A French music curator known as Dimitri ran a channel, first called ChilledCow, that streamed relaxed lo-fi hip hop around the clock over a looping animation of a girl studying, later named Lofi Girl.

  • Lofi Girl’s creators A calm loop, careful curation and a label for independent producers.
  • Listeners Millions who kept the stream on while they studied or worked, and a chat that never slept.

The turning point

The format was copied across YouTube and became a genre of its own. In July 2022 a stream that had run for about two years was taken down by a false copyright claim, prompting a public outcry until YouTube restored it.

My take

Lofi Girl turned YouTube into a radio station people keep on all day. The takedown shows how fragile even the most loved creator assets are when automated systems get it wrong.

09 Musical.ly / 2018 / FormatThe App That Became TikTokA lip-sync app popular with American teenagers was bought and switched off. Its users woke up on TikTok.TikTok · Merged August 2018When Musical.ly was merged into TikTok · legacy factArchival

Shanghai, China, and United States / Short video

Musical.ly and ByteDance, 2017 to 2018

The situation

Musical.ly, founded in Shanghai by Alex Zhu and Luyu Yang, had built a huge young audience in the United States making lip-sync and dance videos. ByteDance had a similar app, Douyin, at home and TikTok abroad.

The move

ByteDance bought Musical.ly in late 2017, in a deal reported at about $1 billion. In August 2018 it merged the apps, moving every Musical.ly account to TikTok.

  • Musical.ly’s creators Millions of young American users and the lip-sync and dance formats they had built.
  • ByteDance TikTok’s recommendation feed, which showed videos from strangers rather than friends.

The turning point

The merger gave TikTok a ready-made Western audience and their formats. Combined with its recommendation feed, it grew into the platform that reshaped short video worldwide.

My take

Musical.ly invented the lip-sync dance culture TikTok became famous for. Its creators moved over and kept going, and most never noticed the change of owner.

10 Adopt Me! / 2019 / CommunityA Game Made by PlayersTwo players built a pet game inside Roblox. At its peak it had more people playing at once than the biggest games on Steam.Roblox · Record 1.6 millionConcurrent players, a Roblox record in 2019Reach only

Global / Gaming

NewFissy and Bethink, 2017 to 2019

The situation

Hit games were made by studios with large budgets. Roblox let anyone build games on its platform and earn from them, but few expected a player-made game to rival commercial releases.

The move

Adopt Me!, created by Roblox developers NewFissy and Bethink, let players adopt, raise and trade pets. Regular updates, like new eggs and pets, brought players back in waves.

  • The developers Simple, social gameplay and steady updates that gave players a reason to return.
  • Roblox Free distribution to millions of young players and a way to earn from in-game purchases.

The turning point

In October 2019 an update pushed Adopt Me! past 1.6 million concurrent players, a record for Roblox. Its team went on to form Uplift Games, a studio built on a player-made hit.

My take

Adopt Me! is the creator economy inside a game. It shows that the next generation of creators may be building worlds rather than filming videos.

Los Angeles, United States / Music and live streaming

D-Nice, 2020

The situation

In March 2020 clubs and venues closed overnight and people were cut off from each other. Instagram Live was used for short chats, not events.

The move

Derrick Jones, known as D-Nice, went live on Instagram and played music for hours from his home, shouting out viewers by name as they joined.

  • D-Nice A DJ’s instinct for a crowd, a deep record collection and warmth on the mic.
  • The audience People desperate for company, sharing the stream with friends until famous names arrived.

The turning point

On 21 March 2020 his stream drew more than 100,000 people at once, including Michelle Obama, Oprah Winfrey and Joe Biden. Club Quarantine became a recurring event and a template for virtual concerts.

My take

Club Quarantine made a social feature into a venue. It also showed that a creator’s role can be to host, making the audience feel seen as they arrive.

12 India’s TikTok creators / 2020 / RulesThe Day TikTok Went Dark in IndiaIndia banned TikTok overnight. Its creators lost their audiences, and every rival raced to take them in.Multi-platform · Banned About 200 millionIndian TikTok users who lost access, as reported · legacy factArchival

India / Short video

Indian creators, 2020

The situation

India was TikTok’s largest market by users, with creators in small towns building audiences in dozens of languages, many earning for the first time.

The move

On 29 June 2020, after border clashes with China, India’s government banned TikTok and 58 other Chinese apps. Creators had to rebuild elsewhere.

  • India’s creators Audiences in many languages, and the skill to rebuild quickly.
  • Rival platforms Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and Indian apps, all launched or pushed in India within months.

The turning point

Instagram launched Reels in India in July 2020 and YouTube tested Shorts there first in September. Many creators rebuilt their audiences on the new formats, and India became the proving ground for TikTok’s rivals.

My take

This case is the creator economy’s biggest stress test. It shows how quickly audiences move when a platform disappears, and how fast rivals arrive to welcome them.

13 Dream / 2020 / CommunityMinecraft ManhuntA faceless creator played Minecraft while friends tried to hunt him down. In 2020 Minecraft was the most watched game on YouTube.YouTube · Breakout year 201 billionMinecraft video views on YouTube in 2020Reach only

United States / Gaming and online video

Dream, 2020

The situation

Minecraft was more than ten years old and seen as a children’s game past its peak. The big gaming names had moved on to newer titles.

The move

Dream, who never showed his face, made Manhunt videos in which he raced to finish the game while friends tried to stop him. He and his friends also ran a role-play server whose story played out across many channels.

  • Dream High-stakes challenges, sharp editing and a mystery about who he was.
  • His friends and fans Collaborations, fan art and clips that carried the story across platforms.

The turning point

In 2020 YouTube said Minecraft videos drew 201 billion views, making it the most watched game on the platform, and Dream was one of the fastest-growing creators of the year.

My take

Dream brought the story back to games. His friends’ server became a shared drama played across dozens of channels, which is a model for collaborative creator storytelling.

14 Snapchat Spotlight creators / 2020 / MonetisationA Million Dollars a DaySnapchat launched a TikTok rival and paid a million dollars a day to the people who posted the best videos.Snapchat · Launched $1 million a dayPaid to Spotlight creators at launch · legacy factArchival

Los Angeles, United States / Short video

Snap, 2020

The situation

TikTok had taken the short-video audience and Snapchat needed creators fast. Its own app had been built for friends, not for public creators.

The move

In November 2020 Snap launched Spotlight, a feed of public short videos, and said it would share more than $1 million a day among the creators of the top videos.

  • Spotlight creators Short videos made for a new feed, from known names and unknowns alike.
  • Snap Cash to get the feed started, and a recommendation system to fill it.

The turning point

Some creators earned hundreds of thousands of dollars in weeks. Snap later said creators had earned more than $250 million on Snapchat in 2021, before the daily prize model was replaced.

My take

Spotlight shows how hard it is to buy a creator ecosystem. Paying for the best videos brought creators in. Keeping them needed an audience Snap was still building.

15 Clubhouse’s hosts / 2021 / LiveThe Room Everyone Wanted to Be InAn invite-only app for live conversation was the hottest thing in tech. Within months the rooms emptied.Clubhouse · Rise and fall 9.6 millionDownloads at the February 2021 peakReach only

San Francisco, United States / Social audio

Clubhouse, 2020 to 2021

The situation

With people stuck at home in 2020 and 2021, live audio offered conversation without cameras. Clubhouse, invitation only, made it feel exclusive.

The move

Hosts ran live rooms on tech, money, music and culture, and celebrities dropped in. Clubhouse launched a creator programme to support its most popular hosts.

  • The hosts Daily shows built on conversation, with no edit and no camera.
  • Clubhouse Scarcity, celebrity appearances and a brief monopoly on social audio.

The turning point

Downloads peaked at about 9.6 million in February 2021, then fell to about 900,000 by April as lockdowns eased and Twitter, Spotify and others copied the format.

My take

Clubhouse is the platform-first case that went wrong in public. Its hosts were creators without a way to keep what they made, because nothing was recorded and nothing could be watched later.

04 / The evidence

What a new format can and can’t prove

Moment, milestone, result

A first upload or a record is a moment. A milestone, like a subscriber count, shows attention. Only a result shows what changed for the creator, the platform or a brand. Each case says which of the three the record supports.

Whose number it is

Many figures come from the platforms, the creators themselves or the press. The source for every outcome is named, so you can judge it. Estimates of creator income are marked as estimates.

Attention is not income

Views, subscribers and concurrent viewers count attention. Paid subscriptions, sales and chart positions get closer to money. The rating says which kind of number a case rests on.

The evidence rating

Every case gets one of five ratings, strongest first: modelled effect, sales trend, survey, reach only and archival. A date or a deal term is marked as a legacy fact so it isn’t read as a result.

The creator comes first

Each case puts the creator at the centre: what they made, what changed because of it, and who else was involved. Brand partnerships appear when the creator drove them.

How cases earn a place

Each case changed what was possible for creators, platforms or brands, and left a public record. Creator stories already told in Brand Turning Points or Partnership Turning Points are not repeated here; The Index links to them.
